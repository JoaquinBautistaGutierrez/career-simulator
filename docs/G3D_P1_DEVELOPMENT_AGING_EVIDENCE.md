## 1. Interpretation boundary

Cross-sectional age performance, longitudinal match measures and exposure
trajectories are kept separate. No observed delta is written as a capability
delta. The evidence can constrain smooth maturation/decline families later, but
does not choose their center, width, amplitude, persistence or noise.

## 2. Published longitudinal evidence

| Study | Population | Position coverage | Use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rey et al. 2022 | 154 La Liga players; 14,092 match observations; 2012/13–2019/20 | central midfielders (34), forwards (17); no GK | within-player physical/technical aging and heterogeneity | selected retained professionals; sample-position imbalance |
| García-Calvo et al. 2023 | 1,037 La Liga players; 36,883 observations; 2015/16–2018/19 | outfield only; GK and <10 minutes excluded | age and season-phase physical patterns | not a complete career panel; excluded sparse exposure |
| Dendir 2016 | top four European leagues, 2010/11–2014/15 | forward/midfield broad groups; no GK-specific estimate | performance-rating peak reference | rating construct, noncommercial license and selection bias |

The published results support smooth, position-dependent aging and reject a
single universal peak age. They do not directly identify the five Development
families or the player-specific offset distribution.

## 4. Within-player exposure-change evidence

Consecutive-season minute-share change is broad even within age bands:

| Age | CM N / mean / SD | GK N / mean / SD | ST N / mean / SD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 18–20 | 1,926 / +.035 / .193 | 1,065 / +.040 / .200 | 1,501 / +.035 / .198 |
| 33–35 | 344 / −.057 / .202 | 575 / −.080 / .326 | 373 / −.098 / .252 |

Both improvement and decline remain possible inside every broad region. These
distributions support later heterogeneity, stagnation, breakthrough and decline
tail diagnostics. They do not supply Development noise parameters because the
observed change also contains club opportunity, health, transfers and selection.

## 5. Bias, censoring and correction needs

1. professional datasets observe survivors and selected opportunities;
2. career exits are right-censored and lower-tier continuation may disappear;
3. minutes select the measured performance population;
4. club changes alter opportunity independently from player development;
5. cross-sectional curves mix cohort, era and survivor effects;
6. published outfield studies do not close GK aging evidence.

Future estimation must retain attrition/censoring indicators, stratify club
change and run sensitivity analyses. No correction factor is invented here.
